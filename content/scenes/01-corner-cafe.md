# Scene 01 — The Corner Café

> Draft content for the first Session. Every question here should be answerable without specialist knowledge or current events (see `CONTEXT.md` → Main question).

## Scene

- **Setting line (shown under the image):** Saturday, 10:40. A busy corner café near the office. Every table is taken, half of them by people on laptops, and a queue is forming at the door.
- **Category:** Everyday decisions
- **Partner tone:** Meeting tone. Polite, brisk, and unconvinced by default. It sounds like a senior colleague who has two minutes.

## Round 1

**Main question:** "The owner is thinking about banning laptops on weekends, from 9 to 2. Good idea or not? What's your call?"

### Follow-up bank

The Partner picks 2–3 of these based on what the learner actually said, and never asks them in a fixed order.

| Follow-up type | Line the Partner can use |
|---|---|
| Why | "What's the single strongest reason?" |
| So what | "Okay, so if she does that — what actually changes for the business?" |
| Counterexample | "But the laptop people buy two coffees and a lunch. Doesn't that kill your argument?" |
| What would change your mind | "What would you need to see to change your mind?" |
| Be specific | "Give me one concrete example of how that plays out on a Saturday." |

### What a strong Answer contains (for the Coach brain)

- **Directness:** a clear position in the first sentence ("Yes, ban them on weekends" / "No — limit them, don't ban them").
- **Structure:** two reasons that don't overlap (for example table turnover versus atmosphere), then a so-what.
- **Reasoning:** names the assumption it rests on (weekend customers are mostly walk-ins), or offers a conditional ("It depends on whether weekend revenue comes from turnover or from long stays").

### Example — a weak first Answer and its Coaching point

> **Learner (first try, 52 s):** "Um, I think, uh, it depends, because, like, some people they need to work, and also the café, they want to make money, so maybe, you know, it's not a bad idea but also maybe some customers will be unhappy, so I think it's like… a balance."

**Coaching point (Directness):** You said "it depends" and "a balance", but you never told me what it depends on, or which way you'd decide. Try again: put your position in the first sentence, then give me two reasons.

> **Learner Redo (34 s):** "I'd do it, but only for the window tables. First, on weekends people are queuing at the door, so a table taken for three hours is lost revenue. Second, the laptop regulars come on weekdays anyway. So she keeps both groups."

**Better version** (in the learner's own position and words, shown after the Redo):

> "Yes — but limit it, don't ban it. On weekends the bottleneck is tables, not customers: there's a queue at the door, and one laptop can hold a table for three hours. Most laptop regulars come on weekdays anyway. So: no laptops at the window tables on weekends from 9 to 2. She gets turnover at peak times and keeps her regulars."

## Round 2

**Main question:** "Different topic. Should cafés stop taking cash completely? Yes or no — and why?"

| Follow-up type | Line the Partner can use |
|---|---|
| Why | "Which reason matters most to you?" |
| So what | "So what's the cost if they're wrong about that?" |
| Counterexample | "What about an older customer who only carries cash?" |
| What would change your mind | "Is there any situation where you'd say the opposite?" |
| Be specific | "Put a number on it — how much time or money are we talking about?" |

**Strong-answer cues:**
- Takes a side, or says "It depends on X" and then names X (for example who the regulars are).
- Treats the counterexample as a cost to weigh, rather than as a reason to switch sides.

## Transfer question

"Last one, quickly. Should restaurants stop taking reservations and go walk-in only?"

This checks whether the Next focus carries over to an unseen question. The Partner gives one sentence of comment, with no Redo.

## Holding phrases offered at onboarding (the learner picks 3)

1. "Short answer: yes. Two reasons."
2. "It depends on one thing — …"
3. "My bottom line is …"
4. "Let me give you my take first, then the why."
5. "There are two options here, and I'd pick …"
6. "Fair challenge. Here's how I see it."

## Thinking patterns (taught one per Session, then faded)

| Pattern | Skeleton | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| Answer first | Position → 2 reasons → so what | You already have an opinion |
| It depends on X | Key variable → default call → what would flip it | You lack information |
| Two options + pick | Option A vs B → pick → one reason | The question is open-ended |
